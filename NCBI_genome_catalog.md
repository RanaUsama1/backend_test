# NCBI genome discovery catalog and metadata guide

Prepared 1 October 2026. **374 organism, strain, and virus-group search entries**, organized into 9 groups, with five NCBI database links per entry.

## Scope and how to use this catalog

This is a broad, manually selected discovery index emphasizing model organisms, major crops and livestock, clinically relevant organisms, and environmental/industrial models. It is not an exhaustive export of NCBI, an official importance ranking, or a count of distinct species. Species, strains, subspecies, virus types, and a few overlapping virus groups are explicitly mixed here for practical searching.

**Evidence distinction:** the 15 assembly records in the next table were checked against NCBI assembly pages or its annotated-genome listing. The larger organism index contains constructed name-based NCBI search links. Every search result set and every organism's coverage across all five databases has NOT been individually audited. Do not interpret a link as confirmation of a current assembly, complete genome, reference designation, or NCBI Gene coverage. Resolve each name to its current TaxID and inspect records before collecting metadata. Virus names and microbial synonyms particularly require checking.

Each organism row links to **Taxonomy, Gene, Nucleotide, Protein, and Assembly** searches. Assembly result/record pages may redirect to NCBI Datasets. Nucleotide searches include partial sequences, transcripts, organelles, and other records, not just whole genomes. A protein hit does not establish a complete annotated assembly. For viruses, use [NCBI Virus](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/labs/virus/vssi/) and the Nucleotide links as the primary genome-discovery route; an Assembly search may be empty. Some viral entries use familiar historical names that NCBI may resolve as synonyms.

## Recommended metadata design

Use separate linked tables. These are proposed fields for your collection, not a promise that every source supplies every value.

| Table / record type | Primary identifier | Useful fields |
|---|---|---|
| Taxonomy | TaxID | Current scientific name, rank, parent TaxID, lineage, synonyms, species TaxID, merged/deleted status |
| Assembly | Assembly accession.version | Organism TaxID, species TaxID, assembly name, GCA/GCF source, paired accession, assembly level, reference status, release date, version status, genome size, contig/scaffold N50, annotation provider and release, BioSample, BioProject |
| Gene | GeneID, where assigned | TaxID, symbol, aliases, description, gene type, locus tag, linked accessions |
| Gene placement / CDS | Assembly accession.version + sequence accession.version + feature identifier | Coordinates, strand, locus tag, GeneID if present, transcript and protein links, annotation release |
| Nucleotide | Accession.version | TaxID, title, molecule type, sequence length, chromosome/segment/plasmid, topology, linked assembly, source features |
| Protein | Accession.version | Product name, amino-acid length, linked CDS/nucleotide, GeneID if present, locus tag, source taxonomy |
| BioSample | BioSample accession | Strain/isolate, host, collection date, geographic location, isolation source, sequencing-related sample descriptors where supplied |

Join TaxID for organism identity, assembly accession.version for assembly identity, and accession.version for sequences. TaxID alone cannot identify an assembly-specific protein set. Prefer assembly annotation and its CDS links when you need the exact proteome for an assembly. Record missing values explicitly. Do not treat unavailable metadata as zero.

GenBank GCA and RefSeq GCF accessions can be paired representations of an assembly. Preserve the pairing and decide whether your analysis counts assemblies or database representations. Do not simply concatenate and count the two inventories. Retain both species TaxID and submitted organism/strain TaxID.

NCBI Gene does not represent every annotated gene in every assembly. For complete assembly-specific feature coverage, use its GFF3 or GenBank annotation; GeneID cross-references may be absent. Some prokaryotic RefSeq WP_ proteins are shared across multiple organisms, so do not force a one-protein-to-one-assembly or one-protein-to-one-species relationship. Viral segment records must be grouped using appropriate isolate/sample metadata rather than counted independently as complete genomes.

## Practical retrieval workflow

1. For a compact initial dataset, choose annotated reference assemblies where available. For maximal discovery, use the broad GenBank inventory as well as RefSeq. Keep your inclusion criteria in the output.
2. Resolve catalog names in Taxonomy, keep current TaxIDs and synonyms, and decide whether to include descendants such as strains.
3. Download assembly metadata before sequences. Select and freeze exact accession versions.
4. Retrieve assembly sequence reports to connect assemblies to chromosome, scaffold, and other nucleotide accessions.
5. Retrieve assembly annotation files for genes/CDS/transcripts and proteins. Enrich with Gene metadata where GeneIDs exist.
6. Join BioSample/BioProject when sample or study metadata are needed.
7. Save retrieval date, software version, query, source database, accession version, and annotation release. Record missing/withdrawn results rather than silently discarding them.

Example commands below follow NCBI Datasets documentation. They are provided for use after installing `datasets` and `dataformat`; they were not executed to harvest data during this catalog preparation.

```bash
# All assembled-genome metadata for an organism (human example)
datasets summary genome taxon 9606 --as-json-lines > human_assemblies.jsonl

# A specific, reproducible assembly version
datasets summary genome accession GCF_000001405.40 --as-json-lines > GRCh38_metadata.jsonl

# Convert selected assembly fields to a tab-delimited table
dataformat tsv genome --inputfile GRCh38_metadata.jsonl --fields accession,assminfo-name,organism-name > GRCh38_metadata.tsv

# Connect assembly records to nucleotide sequence accessions
datasets summary genome accession GCF_000001405.40 --report sequence --as-json-lines > GRCh38_sequences.jsonl
dataformat tsv genome-seq --inputfile GRCh38_sequences.jsonl --fields accession,genbank-seq-acc,refseq-seq-acc,chr-name > GRCh38_sequences.tsv
```

See [NCBI genome metadata guide](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/docs/v2/how-tos/genomes/get-genome-metadata/), [dataformat genome fields](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/docs/v2/reference-docs/command-line/dataformat/tsv/dataformat_tsv_genome/), [download tools](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/docs/v2/download-and-install/), and [genome assembly report schema](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/docs/v2/reference-docs/data-reports/genome-assembly/).

## Other genome categories to include if relevant

- Mitochondrial and plastid genomes: distinguish organelle sequences from nuclear assemblies. See [mitochondrial RefSeq resources](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/refseq/release/mitochondrion/) and [plastid RefSeq resources](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/refseq/release/plastid/).
- Plasmids: preserve the relationship to host/isolate and assembly when known.
- Metagenome-assembled genomes and single-cell assemblies: keep assembly provenance and completeness/contamination information. They may have provisional names and limited Gene coverage.
- Alternative haplotypes, pangenome assemblies, cultivars, and strains: retain separate assembly identities rather than collapsing them to a species name.
- Viroids: use Nucleotide and virus-related resources. Protein and protein-coding gene records are not expected for non-protein-coding viroid genomes.
